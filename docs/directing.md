# Direct for the viewer's changing understanding

This guide helps choose what to show and how to develop it in time. Its subject is the viewer's understanding and response, not a mandatory order of shots. Use the [scenario playbook](scenario-playbook.md) for the film's argument and genre, [combinations](combinations.md) for coordinating actions, and [directed scenes](directed-scenes.md) for the tools that carry them.

## Start with the material and the dramatic question

Identify what the material actually contains: a working behavior, a surprising result, a conflict, a decision, a mechanism, a person or an idea. Choose the question that makes this worth watching. An architecture explanation might ask how two consumers can share one value until the first edit. A product announcement might begin with the result that changes the user's work. A reliability film might follow the moment an expected result fails and the evidence that explains it.

Build development from that question. Reveal information when it changes the viewer's interpretation; give an important result visual weight; let an unresolved relation carry interest into the next scene. The appropriate shape may be result-to-cause, question-to-discovery, chronological action, parallel comparison, escalating examples or an intimate observation. These are alternatives, not slots every film must contain. An expressive opening can create anticipation, while a support walkthrough can immediately show the operation the viewer needs.

## Give each scene a meaningful change

Think about what the viewer knows on entering and what becomes clear by leaving. The scene may reveal an owner, connect a cause to a consequence, locate a control, contrast alternatives, expose a hidden boundary or change the apparent scale of a result. It can also create curiosity, confidence, tension or relief. An effect earns its place by making that change perceptible or giving it the intended emotional weight.

| Viewer needs | Possible staging | Alternative that changes the emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognize an entity | Stable title, shape, color and position; reveal its content | Introduce it through an action, retaining identity as the action completes |
| Understand a change | Hold the baseline, change one state visibly, let the result register | Show the result first, then replay the decisive cause |
| Understand a cause | Action reaches a destination and the destination responds | Compare controlled outcomes, then expose the operation responsible |
| Understand ownership | Keep the original visible while a copy becomes independent | Transfer a temporary value and visibly empty its source |
| Understand a relation | Connection, shared axis, repeated identity or synchronized response | Show two viewpoints of the same object rather than a route |
| Understand location | Whole view, landmark and continuous travel; overview beside detail | A loupe preserves both scales at once |
| Feel scale or breadth | Wall, orbit, marquee or progressive reveal of many real items | A few selected examples, then a measured aggregate with its source |
| Feel a decisive moment | Stronger entrance, cut, sound accent or change of pace | A quiet hold after surrounding movement gives the result weight |

One scene can combine these needs. Showing a copied value beside its highlighted code is one explanation with two views, not two competing stories. When the audience must learn two independent facts, separate their moments or make their hierarchy apparent.

## Keep identity and spatial context across change

Preserve the cues by which a viewer recognizes an object: a title, silhouette, position, color role, direction of travel or shared element across a cut. Replacing a body need not replace its title. A camera should show how a detail belongs to the larger subject; a cut can preserve that connection through a shared object. After navigation or a major rearrangement, provide enough new context to reorient.

Every system interface is a specific invariant: in every format show its whole captured application viewport before zooming, cropping or focusing on a part. Small labels need not be readable in the establishing view; the later reading shot makes the target legible. Do not interpret “whole” as scrolling through an entire document. Use an overview plus detail, continuous zoom, device view or loupe. Automatic portrait conversion preserves this opening for supported material; it cannot reconstruct a missing whole screen.

Other subjects do not require that same sequence. A mechanism may begin on its surprising result; an interview may begin on a face; an abstract idea may begin on an analogy. They still need recognizable entities, meaningful relations and a comprehensible change. Whole-to-detail is one solution to location, not the universal definition of directing.

## Compose the frame for its actual material

Give long code a broad reading area. Pipeline, before-after and ownership compositions place code in a complete row below their objects; diagram-code uses its dedicated code column for shorter snippets and simultaneous visual/code explanation. A full document is context, not something the viewer should read in miniature. Use a selected excerpt, a separate close-up or a loupe when the words matter.

Use perspective, layers and an orbit to reveal depth or introduce a screen, then settle when controls and code must be read. A real screenshot remains real material under a presentation transform. Invented values, reconstructed interfaces and pseudocode still need honest labels. Protect colors when they constitute evidence. A living background can carry atmosphere, while transfer, compare, pointer actions and diagram drawing can carry meaning. Consider both; a glow on every static box does not replace a visible mechanism.

## Stage numbers as evidence

For data, a common scale and visible units can carry the relation more clearly than a transfer metaphor. A chart, paired counters or a measured before/after can show a difference; use copy or transfer only when the source proves that operation. Keep source, date or missing-date qualification near the associated values, inside the filmed target, without making a long qualification the largest object. In Report before-after, the `source` role is the baseline value or originating object, not a bibliographic citation. A source line or short nearby note usually serves provenance better.

## Let time carry the explanation

Leave enough time to recognize the baseline before a change, follow the action and read its result. Narration can anticipate an action, accompany it or interpret it afterwards; choose intentionally. A line about a copied value should coincide with the copy or its completed state, not an unrelated opening screen. Use measured `bN` anchors for final speech timing. Reserve enough arrival time before a dependent action; do not assume a paragraph lasts the standalone Report preview's three seconds.

Reading holds are part of motion design. During a hold, reduce movements that disturb the reading task. `still-stretch` observes unchanged pixels; it does not establish that a pause is poorly directed. Keep a hold that lets the viewer establish an interface, read code or compare a result. Shorten or develop a hold when nothing new is being understood. Do not introduce ambient motion merely to make a warning disappear.

## Stage the same question differently

For “the first edit creates independent ownership”, a causal explanation can begin with the owners and relationship, copy the value beside a code highlight, edit the copy and compare. A reveal can begin on the unexpected independent result, then reconstruct the copy and show why the original survives. A product story can begin with the whole real interface, perform the edit, and move into the mechanism only when the viewer asks why. The code and facts remain the same; the information order, feeling and emphasis differ.

For “one setting changes two outputs”, a simultaneous comparison emphasizes consistency: both results respond to one cue while the source stays visible. A sequential investigation follows the first result, raises the question about the second, then reveals its different resolution path. A UI walkthrough follows the actual control and response before showing those paths. Select the version that serves this audience and material; do not assign a fixed treatment by feature name.

For “a result improved”, a data story can start with a sourced before/after, build its trend and carry the number into the real workflow. An observational story can start with the work, let the improvement occur and give the aggregate at the end. A launch can make that result the opening image and use expressive type and sound to give it weight. Varied staging comes from varied interpretation, not random effects on identical pages.

For real-code architecture explanations, use [architecture films](architecture-films.md) and its buildable example. Develop the concrete operation, stored form, caller/callee and result together with the matching code; midnight/dark is the starting look for this material.
